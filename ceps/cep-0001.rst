What is a CEP?
==============

Similar to PEP (Python Enhancement Proposal), CEP stands for CFMS Enhancement 
Proposal.  A CEP is a design document providing information to the CFMS 
community, or describing a new feature for CFMS or its processes or 
environment.  The CEP should provide a concise technical specification of 
the feature and a rationale for the feature.

We intend CEPs to be the primary mechanisms for proposing major new
features, for collecting community input on an issue, and for
documenting the design decisions that have gone into CFMS.  The CEP
author is responsible for building consensus within the community and
documenting dissenting opinions.

Because the CEPs are maintained as text files in a versioned
repository, their revision history is the historical record of the
feature proposal. This historical record is available by the normal git
commands for retrieving older revisions, and can also be browsed
`on GitHub <https://github.com/cfms-dev/ceps>`__.


CEP Audience
============

The typical primary audience for CEPs consists of the core developers of the 
CFMS server-side implementation, and potentially also developers of clients 
based on the CFMS protocol.

However, other parts of the CFMS community may also choose to use the process
(particularly for Informational CEPs) to document expected API conventions and
to manage complex design coordination problems that require collaboration across
multiple projects.


CEP Types
=========

Similar to PEP, There are three kinds of CEP:

1. A **Standards Track** CEP describes a new feature or implementation
   for CFMS.

2. An **Informational** CEP describes a CFMS design issue, or
   provides general guidelines or information to the CFMS community,
   but does not propose a new feature.  Informational CEPs do not
   necessarily represent a CFMS community consensus or
   recommendation, so users and implementers are free to ignore
   Informational CEPs or follow their advice.

3. A **Process** CEP describes a process surrounding CFMS, or
   proposes a change to (or an event in) a process.  Process CEPs are
   like Standards Track CEPs but apply to areas other than the CFMS
   project itself.  They may propose an implementation, but not to
   CFMS's codebase; they often require community consensus; unlike
   Informational CEPs, they are more than recommendations, and users
   are typically not free to ignore them.  Examples include
   procedures, guidelines, changes to the decision-making process, and
   changes to the tools or environment used in CFMS development.
   Any meta-CEP is also considered a Process CEP.
